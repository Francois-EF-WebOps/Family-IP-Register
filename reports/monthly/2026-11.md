## 3. Research ideas captured — not established conclusions

### Nazareth / submerged-island hypotheses

A conversation explored three competing possibilities:
1. Nazareth in the relevant narrative may refer to a submerged island or place.
2. The story may originate from a wider region than conventional chronology assumes.
3. The narrative may preserve material connected to that region.

The discussion also raised tectonic activity, changing coastlines, human settlement timelines and the limits of calendar-based chronology. These remain hypotheses and research questions, not established history. Any project record must compare geological dating, archaeological evidence, textual transmission, place-name history and independent chronology. Do not state that humans lived in Antarctica or that a specific submerged island has been identified without credible evidence.

**Disposition:** retain as an exploratory research lead only; no implementation ticket created.

### Content and identity continuity

The conversations reinforced the principle that content should preserve narrative continuity, and that recurring characters, places, styles, personas and commercial properties should be governed by stable source records.

**Disposition:** apply through existing identity/provenance systems; do not create a second character or brand registry.
