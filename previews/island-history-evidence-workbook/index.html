Island History Evidence Workbook

A practical notebook for asking better historical questions and showing how you know.

This four-page A4 workbook helps readers separate sources, claims, evidence and interpretation. It is designed for use with real records and sources—not to supply pre-written historical conclusions.

Core distinction
A source is something we can examine. A claim is something said about the past. Evidence is what the source supports. Interpretation explains what it may mean. These are related, but they are not interchangeable.
Sample activity

Start with a question

My topic / place

Time period (and why this boundary?)

My research question

What would count as a useful answer?
Whose perspectives might be missing?

The complete edition contains four pages covering question framing, source records, claim audits, synthesis and a review checklist.

A second example

Test what a claim says

What exact evidence supports this claim?
Is the source direct, second-hand or unclear?
What other source could confirm or challenge it?
What is known, inferred, disputed or unknown?

Prefer precise language: “the record states…”, “this source suggests…”, or “the available evidence does not establish…”. Do not turn an absence of evidence into proof that something never happened.
