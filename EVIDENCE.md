# Evidence and verification standard

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer primary, dated records:

1. Signed agreements, assignments, employment/client contracts, contributor agreements, and written permissions.
2. Original dated source files, Git commits, release tags, issue/PR discussions, and repository licence history.
3. Emails or other contemporaneous written records establishing who contributed what and under which terms.
4. Official platform records, registrations, invoices, contracts, and dated public releases.
5. Later recollections or summaries, clearly labelled as recollections rather than primary evidence.

A Git commit proves that a change was recorded under an account at a time; it does not alone prove the legal identity of the author, inventorship, or ownership.

## Record each claim separately

For every material claim, record:
- Claim ID and related asset ID.
- Exact claim, written narrowly.
- Status: verified / provisional / disputed / unknown.
- Evidence reference and date.
- What the evidence does and does not establish.
- Reviewer and review date.

Do not merge facts, interpretations, and legal conclusions into one statement.

## Disclosure record

For each potentially protectable technical asset, record:
- Date and timezone, if known.
- Public URL or event.
- Commit, release, document, talk, demonstration, sale, offer, or other disclosure.
- Audience and access conditions.
- Exact features or technical details disclosed.
- Whether the disclosure was confidential or public.
- Supporting evidence.

A later private setting or deletion does not undo a prior public disclosure. Patent grace periods and novelty rules vary by jurisdiction; do not assume that a disclosure is harmless worldwide.

## Licence and third-party rights

Record the exact licence file and commit/release at which it appeared. Check:
- Code dependencies and their licences.
- Datasets, archives, images, maps, fonts, music, recordings, and generated media.
- Platform, model, API, and SDK terms.
- Contributor permissions and community consent.
- Attribution, share-alike, non-commercial, or other restrictions.

Do not assume a repository licence grants rights to material the repository author did not own or have authority to license.

## Public/private boundary

Never publish identity documents, private addresses, bank or payment information, passwords, API keys, private family records, children's information, confidential correspondence, unpublished source data, or confidential invention details here.

A public entry may describe that evidence exists and identify a private evidence custodian/location without exposing the evidence itself.
